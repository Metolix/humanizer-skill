# Research Notes

There is no single AI-writing fingerprint. Detection research uses statistical, linguistic, stylistic, and model-specific signals. Results can change with model choice, prompting, editing, translation, genre, and author style.

## Practical categories

Humanizer reviews clusters involving predictable lexical choices, low structural variation, repetitive discourse markers, generic framing, excessive abstraction, formulaic organization, repeated rhetorical templates, uniform rhythm, and semantic repetition.

## What is not proof

A single punctuation mark, transition word, polished sentence, heading, bullet list, or vocabulary choice cannot establish AI authorship. Human writers use these features too.

## Research policy

Prefer primary and peer-reviewed sources. Record publication date, source URL, method, dataset, and limitations when adding evidence. Distinguish research findings from vendor marketing claims.

Useful starting points for maintainers include GLTR, DetectGPT, OpenAI's historical AI classifier documentation, and published work on detector false positives and robustness. These sources should be rechecked before making current claims because model and detector behavior changes.

## Project position

Humanizer does not promise to make text undetectable. It targets writing quality and authentic voice. That makes the skill less brittle than a detector-specific phrase blacklist and avoids treating probabilistic classifiers as authorship proof.
